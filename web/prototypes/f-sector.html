Prototype F — Sector Plates

Thesis: four drums, one per donor sector, each at its own screen angle. A district's colour is its donor mix and its total ink is its money. Nothing here is sampled from a ramp: the tone is whatever four overprinting screens leave on the paper, which is what a four-colour riso actually does. D is the control — money only — and F is the same money carrying a second, categorical channel for free.

Why there are four plates and not three. The round-2 plan named three: Corporate, Trade Association, Labor. Measured on the 2026 artifact, those three cover only 61.92% of a district's PAC dollars on average (median 66.62%), and two districts have none of them at all. The remainder is Leadership PAC ($38.1M), Membership ($27.9M), Unclassified ($22.0M), Candidate Committee ($10.2M) and a long tail. Three plates alone would print a district's total ink as 62% of its money while the page claimed that total ink is the money — the same class of error as this project's standing rule that itemized is never presented as total. So a fourth, deliberately neutral, low-chroma plate carries the remainder and is labelled All other sectors in the legend. It is a remainder, never a fifth category with a meaning of its own.

The three named plates are not the top three by dollars, and that is on purpose. Nationally in 2026 the order is Corporate $117.7M · Trade Association $59.0M · Leadership PAC $38.1M · Labor $32.8M · Membership $27.9M. Leadership PAC is third and it goes into the neutral plate; Labor is fourth and gets a drum. Corporate / trade / labour is the distinction this map exists to draw, and it survives a cycle in which labour money happens to be smaller than one sector of intra-party transfers. SECTOR_ORDER in inks.js is measured and is 2024's order; it is not what picks these plates, so nobody should "fix" this later by re-deriving the plates from it.

Total ink is exactly the money, whatever the mix. proportionalPlates gives plate i a coverage of 1 − (1 − C)si, so the paper left in the halftone cell composites to exactly (1 − C) — the money — regardless of how one-sided the district is. A district that is all corporate runs the full 0.20 → 0.88 on one drum; an evenly-mixed one lands at 0.412 on each of four. Both leave the same 12% of the cell as paper at the top of the range, which is what keeps the certainty screen legible on the districts with the most money, and what makes D, E and F show the same amount of ink for the same dollars.

Zoom: a blow-up is a NEW PLATE, re-screened coarser (3.6px cells nationally, 8px in a state), not a camera move over the same one. The certainty ratios scale with it, so a superseded district stays 2.33× coarser than a current one at either size.

Colour is provisional. The four inks are referenced by name from F.plates and are still placeholders: they have not been through validate_palette.js, and in particular their overprints have not been checked for mutual distinguishability, which is the check F needs and D does not.
