Prototype B — Plate

Thesis: screen frequency encodes certainty. Fine screen = the map that governs; coarse, visibly broken screen = a district drawn off a map that is no longer the law. Registration stays perfect, so no ink crosses a district border — the objection that sinks misregistration-as-certainty does not apply here.

Cost: one WebGL2 draw call over a static mesh (triangulated once on layout, never per frame), plus a Canvas2D pass for the keylines. Kubelka-Munk overprint is ~20 lines of GLSL and replaces mix-blend-mode, which silently stops compositing on promoted layers in WebKit.

Port risk: this is the expensive one. On MapLibre it becomes a CustomLayerInterface that has to use projectTile() and restore the stencil state, or district outlines and labels drop out. That risk is real and is the thing to weigh against how much better it looks.
