Prototype E — Tilt

Thesis: two channels on D's three drums. Lightness is money — the composite paper left in the halftone cell is exactly the money, whatever the mix — and hue is the party tilt of the recipient. Red drum, neutral grey drum, blue drum, and a district's ink is split between them in proportion to where its money actually went. A diverging scale needs a neutral midpoint rather than a third hue, so the middle drum is grey: "even" is the absence of tilt, not a category standing beside R and D.

The argument for E does not survive being measured. Round 2 justified this prototype by noting that 2026 House money is near-even nationally — — — and concluding that district-level variation would therefore be real rather than a one-sided wash. At district level it is a wash on both sides at once. The tilt histogram over the — districts that have any major-party money at all:

Districts by party tilt of the money they received, 2026 cycle.

— sit in the two extreme bins, and — are within 20 points of even. The near-even national total is two one-sided halves cancelling out. E therefore renders as a near-binary two-colour map with an almost empty middle, and the neutral drum the whole encoding is built around is inked by almost nothing.

That is a finding rather than a fault. PAC money tracks the incumbent's party almost exactly, and E is the prototype that shows it — the map is a party map with a money channel laid over it. The neutral drum stays. Dropping it because it is rarely inked would make the few genuinely even districts print as a hue, which is the one thing a diverging scale may not do, and it would delete the very thing the finding is measured against. Nothing here was re-scaled to manufacture a gradient the data does not contain.

No major-party money is not an even split. Both land on the neutral drum, because there is nowhere else for them to land. They are told apart in words — in the tooltip, in the sheet and in the table — and never by colour alone. —
